You write cards occasionally and review them daily, so reviewing runs in its own window. **Flashcards** is installed with the editor, uses the same vaults and the same folders, and opens showing what is due today. A person who only ever answers cards need not know an editor exists.

## What is due

<Shot
  name="decks"
  alt="The flashcards window: a year of answering as a grid of weeks, the goals of the vault, and the decks with what is due in each today."
  caption="The past year, the goals of the vault, and what is due in each deck today."
/>

It opens with the vaults it knows and what is due in each; pick one and you get its decks. Reviewing the whole vault is the usual choice, and it is the one filled button. A deck below it is for when you want to review only that deck.

The grid at the top shows what you answered on each day, followed by the coming weeks, so you can see next month's workload in advance.

There is one tile for each [preset](#a-preset) that schedules something. It shows the preset's goal, how far through today it is, and how much work a session on it would involve. A deck shows how much of it is learned and how much is due, or why nothing is due: its preset's day is used up, or today's work contains none of its cards.

## A session

<Shot
  name="session"
  alt="The flashcards window: one card with its answer showing and the four ways of answering it under it."
  caption="One card, four answers, and the interval each one gives."
/>

One card at a time, and four ways of answering it. Under each answer is the time until the card is shown again, so you choose an answer by choosing that interval.

|                             |                         |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| <kbd>Space</kbd>            | show the answer         |
| <kbd>1</kbd> – <kbd>4</kbd> | Again, Hard, Good, Easy |
| <kbd>U</kbd>                | undo the last answer    |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd>              | leave the session       |

You can look further without leaving the session: **Read** opens the note linked to the deck beside the card, and **Ask** opens the agent beside it. A link inside a card opens the reader at the note it points to.

Cards are only read and answered here. To fix a badly written card, use the editor.

## A preset

<Shot
  name="preset"
  alt="The numen window: a preset, with the curve of its goal and the settings it schedules by under it."
  caption="Change the one setting and the curve shows what that pace costs and what it gives you."
/>

How much a day holds is a note in the vault, not a setting on the machine: two vaults can have different daily workloads, and one vault can hold subjects that need different daily loads. A preset is a note of `type: preset`, and its settings are frontmatter keys.

```markdown
---
type: preset
goal: minutes_a_day
minutes_a_day: 20
new_a_day: 8
reviews_a_day: 45
retention: 0.87
load:
  sat: 50
  sun: 0
even_load: true
---

# Sanskrit

Grammar and vocabulary. Three decks point here.
```

A [deck](/cards/) names its preset with a link, and several decks may point at one. A deck naming no preset is scheduled by the defaults.

Open a preset in the editor and it opens in its own tab: one control at the top, the curve of its goal, and the settings it schedules by under it. Change the knob and the curve shows what that pace requires of you, what it gives, and when the backlog is cleared. Nothing is saved while you move it.

### The goal

`goal` selects which of three budgets controls the preset, and only that budget ends a day.

|                 |                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `minutes_a_day` | the day ends when the time spent on answers reaches the limit.                                                                       |
| `retention`     | the target sets the intervals, and the two card counts end the day.                                                                  |
| `by_date`       | the day contains what has to be reviewed to have the material learned by that date. Neither the minutes nor the counts cut it short. |

A setting the goal does not use keeps its value and has no effect. If you set a card limit last month and then choose a goal that ignores it, the limit is still there.

A controlling budget of zero pauses the preset: it schedules nothing, and every deck pointing at it stops. To pause one deck, give it a preset of its own.

### What counts as learned

`learned` sets the rule the preset counts by. Under `interval` a card is learned once its interval is long: it reaches `interval` days. Under `retention` it is learned while the chance of recalling it today is at or above the target.

Learned is a state, not a milestone: a lapse takes a card back out of it.

A date goal targets this rule, checked on the date it names. The pace depends on the rule, so the same material and the same date give a different pace under each rule. What no pace can reach (for example, a card added two weeks before a date by which its interval must be three weeks) is counted and reported, and the date is not changed for it.

### What a day is spent on

`counts: cards` is the default: a card counts against the day the first time it is answered that day, and every further showing is free. `counts: shows` spends a slot on every showing, which suits a subject whose cards need four steps to be learned. Minutes are spent on every showing either way.

`backlog` is the share of a day spent on overdue cards before new cards are offered, in per cent. 100 means overdue cards first, 0 means new cards first. A side that runs short leaves the rest of the day to the other, so a day is never left unspent.

### The days of the week

`load` is the share of the daily load that each day of the week gets, in per cent, under the first three letters of its name. A day the preset does not name gets the whole load, and a day set to 0 pauses that one day.

`even_load` sets whether a card may be moved from the day it became due. With it on, the day within the tolerance around the interval is chosen by weight, and a day with a lighter load is more likely to be chosen. With it off the card stays on the day the scheduler chose, and a day that cannot accept it picks it up the next morning.

### Several decks, one day

A preset's day is divided among the decks it schedules before any of it is used, in proportion to what is due in each. Adding a deck does not add to the day's work; it divides the same work among more decks. Raising the work is raising the budget.

So the row on the overview and the session over one deck are the same number, and reviewing your decks in any order uses the same daily budget.

## When a day begins

The hour a day of review begins at is `review.day_start_time` in [the settings file](/settings/) — a setting that depends on your clock rather than on a subject. An answer given before it counts toward the day before.

## What it never touches

Flashcards reads the vault list and never writes it, so the list stays as the editor last saved it. It writes into your decks in one case only: a deck holding a card with no card id is written once when you sit down to that vault, which gives every card in it a [card id](/cards/#a-deck) to record answers against.
