A flashcard is two things that change at different rates. The shape — which fields it has, and how they are laid out on the side you are shown — is settled once and used a thousand times. The filling is what you typed, and there is a great deal of it. numen keeps them in two kinds of note: a **stencil** is the shape, a **deck** is the filling.

Both are markdown in your own folder, with `type:` in the frontmatter saying which they are. Nothing is hidden in a database you cannot open.

## A stencil

<Shot
  name="stencil"
  alt="The numen window: a stencil naming four fields, and the faces that show them, each written beside its preview."
  caption="The fields at the top, and under them the faces — what is asked, and what answers it."
/>

A stencil names its **fields** in the frontmatter, in the order you are asked for them, and its **faces** in the body. A face is a front and a back with `{{Field}}` standing where a value goes.

```markdown
---
type: stencil
fields:
  - Word
  - Reading
  - Meaning
---

## Recognise

### Front

<p>{{Word}} — <i>{{Reading}}</i></p>

### Back

<p><b>Meaning:</b> {{Meaning}}</p>
```

One stencil can carry several faces, and each face becomes a card of its own to answer: recognise it, produce it, read it aloud. A face naming a field the stencil does not declare is reported against the stencil, and the rest of it is read as usual.

**A value is HTML, and so is the face it is laid into.** A card is the one thing in a vault that is not markdown: what you write there is drawn as the markup it is, so rich text on a card is written in tags. The file around it is still markdown, and the headings that cut it into cards and fields are markdown's.

## A deck

<Shot
  name="deck"
  alt="The numen window: a deck of Sanskrit roots as a grid of cards under their sections."
  caption="The cards as a grid, under the headings you filed them in, and the preset that schedules them at the top."
/>

A deck is a note of `type: deck`. A first-level heading opens a **section**; a second-level heading is a **card**; a third-level heading is one of its fields.

```markdown
---
type: deck
---

# Verbs of going

## gam ^k7m2xq9fzp

[[Root]]

### Root

gam

### Meaning

to go, to move
```

The wikilink under a card names the stencil that cuts it. A card's heading is its first field, read back and cut to one line: throw it away and numen writes it again from the field.

The `^` and ten characters at the end of the heading are the card's **mark**, which is what the card is for as long as it exists. It is written when the card is made, and a card you typed in by hand is given one the next time numen writes the file. The mark does not know which deck it is in: move a card to another deck, or to another vault, and it is the same card with the same history.

A deck need not have sections, and cards may stand before the first one. Text between a section's heading and its first card is yours — nothing lays it out and nothing reads it, and a write puts it back where it was.

## Making them

**New deck** and **New stencil** are on any row of the [files](/files/), and in the [commands](/commands/). Both make the file in the folder you asked on.

In a deck, the plus at the end of a run makes a card there, cut by the stencil you pick. Drag a card to move it, between sections or within one; drag a field in a stencil to change the order you are asked for them. A section is made at the end of the deck, and taking one away takes away its heading and nothing else — its cards stay where they stand.

The [agent](/agent/) can do all of it too: make a deck or a stencil, add cards to a deck from a note you point it at, edit one, take one away, open a section, and rename a field across a stencil and everything cut by it. What it writes is the same markdown you would have typed, in your own folder.

## What is reported

A deck and a stencil are read strictly, and what numen would not guess at is drawn on the card or the field it stands against rather than at the top of the file:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| A card under no stencil | the wikilink is missing, or points at a note that is not a stencil. |
| A card naming a field the stencil does not have | the value is kept and the card says so. |
| A field written twice under one card | numen will not choose which is meant. |
| Two cards carrying one mark | a card copied by hand. Both are shown, each marked. |

Nothing you wrote is thrown away or rewritten to make a file valid. The file stays as you left it and the window says what it found.

## Scheduling them

A deck names the [preset](/review/#a-preset) that schedules it with a link of `type: preset` — the line at the top of the deck tab is where you pick one. A deck naming no preset is scheduled by the defaults.

A session over your cards is a [window of its own](/review/).

## Not searched by their text

A deck and a stencil are not indexed as prose. A card's heading reaches the index as a heading, and that is what a search answers with — the fields, the faces and the marks are the card's own bookkeeping and stay out of your results.
