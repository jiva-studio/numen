<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> searches the vault. One field, and the results appear in
three bands:

|             |                                                                           |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Names**   | notes whose title matches what you typed.                                 |
| **Text**    | notes whose words match, and the headings inside them.                    |
| **Meaning** | notes that are about what you typed, whether or not they use those words. |

Type a few letters and the first band shows results immediately. The other two follow as they are computed. What you typed is marked in each result, and a band that has found nothing is not shown. While any band is still working, a spinner turns at the right end of the field; when every band has answered and none found anything, the list says _No results for “…”_ with what you typed, under a search icon.

## What you can do with a result

Choose it to open the note. A result found at a particular heading opens the note _at that
heading_, a result inside a [document](/documents/) opens the document at the passage the
words were found in, and a result inside a [recording](/recordings/) opens the recording at the
second they were spoken.

<kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move through the list, <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens the highlighted result,
and
<kbd>Escape</kbd> closes the panel.

**Go to note** — <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>G</kbd> — is the shorter version: names
only, for when you know exactly which note you want.

**Go to the note the vault opens with** opens the first note of the vault, which is
the note numen shows when no other note has been chosen. Use it to return to the start.

## What is searched

Four things, separately, which is why a result can point at a place and not only at a file:

|            |                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Titles     | what a note is called.                                                                                                                           |
| Headings   | the headings inside it, so a result opens the note at the right one.                                                                             |
| The text   | of notes, of every [document](/documents/) the vault holds, and of what was spoken in every [recording](/recordings/) that has been transcribed. |
| Part names | the chapters and sections of a book, so a result names the part it was found in.                                                                 |

## Creating the note you typed

As soon as you type, the actions of every result include _Create note “…”_ with the words you typed. Open the actions of a result to see it. If a note was open at the time, the actions also offer to create it as a **child**, as a **parent** or as a **jump** from that note.

A search is often a note you have not written yet, so this is the quickest way to go from noticing that to having the note. The actions belong to results, so a search that finds nothing has no result to open them on; use **Create note** from the commands for that.

## Search by meaning

The first two bands are words: they need no model, no account and no network, and they work on
a vault the moment it is scanned.

The third band is different. It needs a model to read your notes, and numen downloads a small
one to your machine on first use. It runs there, and nothing you write leaves the computer.
Until that has happened you will see the task _Search by words only_ in the title bar, and the Meaning
band will say the vault has not been read for meaning yet.

A large vault takes a while to be read the first time. It runs in the background, the rest of
the search works throughout, and the title bar shows its progress.

If you would rather have no such model at all — or would rather one ran on a service you already
pay for — that is a [setting](/settings/).
