Tidal Mills of the Rance Estuary

A tidal mill stored seawater behind a sluice gate at high tide and ground grain while the water ran back out through the wheel. The millers of the Rance estuary worked by the moon, starting at whatever hour the ebb began, by day or by night.

The pond behind the dam filled twice a day. A good miller could grind for six hours on one ebb, and the largest mills on the estuary kept eight pairs of millstones turning at once.

Most of the mills closed when steam mills in the towns undercut them, but the stone buildings still stand on the dams, and one of them has been restored to grind flour for visitors in the summer.

Published by the estuary history society.
